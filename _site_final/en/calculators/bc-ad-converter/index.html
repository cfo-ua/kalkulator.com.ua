Understanding Dating Systems:

  • BC (Before Christ) — Years before the birth of Jesus Christ (counted backwards)
  • AD (Anno Domini) — Years after the birth of Jesus Christ (modern system)
  • Year Zero — Does not exist in historical chronology; 1 BC is followed by 1 AD
  • BCE/CE — Secular equivalents meaning "Before Common Era" and "Common Era"

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is there no year zero in the BC/AD system?
The BC/AD system was created by the monk Dionysius Exiguus in the 6th century, before the concept of zero was known in Europe. Therefore, 1 BC is immediately followed by 1 AD.
How do you calculate time spans across BC and AD?
When calculating between BC and AD years, you must account for the missing year zero. For example, from 5 BC to 5 AD is 9 years, not 10 years.
Are there other dating systems besides BC/AD?
Yes, including the Julian calendar, Hebrew calendar, Islamic Hijri calendar, Chinese calendar, and many others. BC/AD is most common in Western and Christian contexts.
What do BCE and CE mean?
BCE (Before Common Era) and CE (Common Era) are secular equivalents of BC and AD, used to avoid religious references while maintaining the same chronological system.
How accurate are very ancient dates?
Dates older than 2000 years may have uncertainties due to limited historical records and dating methods. Archaeological techniques like carbon dating help verify ancient chronologies.
Who established the BC/AD dating system?
The system was devised by Dionysius Exiguus in 525 AD to calculate Easter dates. It gradually became the standard chronological system in Christian Europe and later worldwide.
How do historians date events before written records?
Historians use archaeological methods like radiocarbon dating, dendrochronology (tree rings), stratigraphy, and other scientific techniques to establish chronologies for prehistoric periods.
Is the BC/AD system used worldwide?
While BC/AD (or BCE/CE) is widely used internationally, many cultures maintain their traditional calendars alongside it. For example, Japan uses both the Western calendar and their own era system.
