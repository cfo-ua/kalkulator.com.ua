Parking Ratio Calculator Online

This calculator helps you determine the required number of parking spaces for different types of buildings according to building codes and local planning regulations.

What is a parking ratio?

A parking ratio is a regulatory standard that defines the number of parking spaces required per unit of building area or number of visitors. The ratio depends on:

  • Building type (office, shopping center, residential, etc.)
  • Location (city center, residential area, suburbs)
  • Regional car ownership levels
  • Public transportation accessibility

Typical parking coefficients:

  • Office buildings: 3-6 spaces per 100 m² of total area
  • Shopping centers: 15-25 spaces per 100 m² of retail area
  • Residential buildings: 1-2 spaces per apartment
  • Restaurants: 10-15 spaces per 100 m² of dining area
  • Hotels: 1 space per 2-3 rooms

The calculator considers various factors and provides recommendations for the optimal number of parking spaces for your project.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a parking ratio?
It's a regulatory standard for the number of parking spaces required for a specific building type or facility. Calculated as the ratio of spaces to area or number of visitors.
What factors affect the parking ratio?
Building type, location, car ownership levels, public transportation access, operating hours, and facility specifics all influence parking requirements.
Are parking standards mandatory?
Yes, building codes and local planning regulations typically require minimum parking space provisions to obtain construction permits and approvals.
How to calculate parking for mixed-use buildings?
For mixed-use buildings, ratios are summed for each use type, accounting for their respective areas or capacities.
What if there's insufficient space?
Consider multi-level parking, underground garages, leasing adjacent lots, or seeking variance approvals from planning authorities.
How does location affect the ratio?
City centers may have reduced requirements due to public transit, while suburban areas may require more spaces due to car dependency.
