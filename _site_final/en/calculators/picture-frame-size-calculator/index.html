Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure artwork for framing?
Measure the exact dimensions of the image area only (excluding any existing frame). For canvas paintings, measure the visible painted area. For photos, measure the print size. Always add 2-3mm clearance for easy fitting.
What's the optimal mat width for different artwork sizes?
Standard mat width is 2-3 inches (5-8cm) for small works under 12 inches, and 3-6 inches (8-15cm) for larger pieces. The bottom mat border is traditionally 0.5-1cm wider than the top and sides.
Do I need a mat for all types of artwork?
Mats aren't required but are recommended for photographs, watercolors, and works on paper. They create visual breathing space and protect the artwork from direct contact with glass.
How do I choose the right mat color?
Safest choices are white, cream, or light gray. Colored mats should complement, not compete with the artwork. Consider the artwork's dominant colors and overall tone.
What materials are best for valuable artwork?
Use archival-quality materials: acid-free mat board and UV-protective glass or acrylic. For valuable pieces, consider museum-quality materials and professional conservation framing.
What's the proper hanging height for framed artwork?
The center of the artwork should be at eye level, typically 57-60 inches (145-152cm) from the floor. For groupings, maintain a consistent center line across all pieces.
How do I calculate spacing between multiple frames?
Optimal spacing is 2-6 inches (5-15cm) between frames, depending on their size. Larger artworks can have more space between them, while smaller pieces look better closer together.
What frame width works best for different artwork styles?
Thin frames (0.5-1 inch) suit modern, minimalist pieces. Medium frames (1-2 inches) work for most artwork. Wide frames (2+ inches) complement traditional or bold pieces but can overwhelm small works.
