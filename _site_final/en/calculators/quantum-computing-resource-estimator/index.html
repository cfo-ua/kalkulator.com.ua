🖥️ Quantum Hardware Platforms

🌀 Superconducting Qubits:

  • Gate Time: 10-100 nanoseconds
  • Coherence: 10-100 microseconds
  • Examples: IBM Quantum, Google Sycamore, Rigetti
  • Advantages: Fast gates, good connectivity

⚛️ Trapped Ion Qubits:

  • Gate Time: 1-100 microseconds
  • Coherence: Seconds to minutes
  • Examples: IonQ, Honeywell, Alpine Quantum
  • Advantages: High fidelity, full connectivity

💡 Photonic Qubits:

  • Gate Time: Nanoseconds
  • Coherence: Effectively infinite
  • Examples: PsiQuantum, Xanadu
  • Advantages: Room temperature, networking capable

🎯 Quantum Error Correction

  • 🔧 Surface Code: 1000-10,000 physical qubits per logical qubit
  • 🌈 Color Code: Higher threshold but more complex
  • 🔀 Concatenated Codes: Layered error correction
  • 📊 Threshold: Error rates below 0.1-1% needed
  • ⚖️ Overhead: 100x to 1000x physical qubit cost

Frequently Asked Questions

How many qubits do I need for my quantum algorithm?
Qubit requirements depend on your problem size and desired precision. Most practical applications need 50-1000 logical qubits, which translates to 50,000-1,000,000 physical qubits with error correction.
What's the difference between logical and physical qubits?
Logical qubits are the 'perfect' qubits your algorithm uses. Physical qubits are the actual hardware qubits needed to implement one logical qubit with error correction - typically 100-10,000 physical qubits per logical qubit.
When will quantum computers be practical for real applications?
Some applications like optimization and simulation may become practical in 5-10 years with 100-1000 logical qubits. Cryptographically relevant quantum computers may require 10-20 years and millions of physical qubits.
How do I choose between quantum hardware platforms?
Superconducting qubits offer fast operations, trapped ions provide high fidelity, and photonics enable networking. Choose based on your algorithm's requirements for speed, accuracy, and connectivity.
What error rates are needed for practical quantum computing?
For fault-tolerant quantum computing, physical qubit error rates need to be below 0.1-1% (the error correction threshold). Current systems have error rates of 0.1-1%, approaching this threshold.
Can I run quantum algorithms on classical computers?
Yes, for small problems (up to ~30-40 qubits). Quantum simulators can model quantum algorithms classically, but they become exponentially slow as qubit count increases.
How much does quantum computing cost?
Cloud quantum computing costs $0.001-$0.1 per quantum operation. Building quantum computers costs millions to billions of dollars. Cloud access is most cost-effective for research and development.
What programming languages are used for quantum computing?
Popular quantum programming languages include Qiskit (Python), Cirq (Python), Q# (Microsoft), and OpenQASM. Most are based on Python for ease of use and integration with classical computing.
