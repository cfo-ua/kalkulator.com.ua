    <h2>Pet Calorie Calculator</h2>
    <p>Calculate the precise daily calorie requirements for your dog, cat, or other pets with our comprehensive <strong>pet calorie calculator</strong>. Get personalized feeding recommendations based on your pet's weight, age, activity level, and health status.</p>

    <h3>🍽️ How Pet Calorie Calculation Works</h3>
    <p>Our calculator uses veterinary-approved formulas to determine your pet's daily energy needs:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>⚖️ Resting Energy Requirement (RER):</strong> Base metabolic energy needs</li>
      <li><strong>🏃 Daily Energy Requirement (DER):</strong> Total calories including activity</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Life Stage Factors:</strong> Adjustments for puppies, kittens, seniors</li>
      <li><strong>🏥 Health Considerations:</strong> Modifications for medical conditions</li>
      <li><strong>🎯 Activity Level:</strong> Sedentary to very active lifestyle adjustments</li>
      <li><strong>🔄 Weight Management:</strong> Calorie adjustments for weight loss/gain</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📏 Standard Calorie Formulas</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Dogs (RER):</strong> 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75</li>
      <li><strong>Cats (RER):</strong> 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75</li>
      <li><strong>Small pets:</strong> Adjusted formulas for rabbits, guinea pigs, etc.</li>
      <li><strong>Activity multipliers:</strong> 1.2-2.0 depending on lifestyle and life stage</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎯 Activity Level Guidelines</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🛋️ Sedentary (1.2-1.4x):</strong> Indoor pets, minimal exercise, senior pets</li>
      <li><strong>🚶 Light Activity (1.4-1.6x):</strong> Short daily walks, indoor/outdoor cats</li>
      <li><strong>🏃 Moderate Activity (1.6-1.8x):</strong> Regular walks, active play, normal adult pets</li>
      <li><strong>⚡ High Activity (1.8-2.0x):</strong> Working dogs, very active pets, multiple daily exercises</li>
      <li><strong>🏋️ Very High Activity (2.0-2.5x):</strong> Working/sporting dogs, lactating females</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🔄 Life Stage Multipliers</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🐶 Growing Puppies:</strong> 2.0-3.0x RER (depending on age)</li>
      <li><strong>🐱 Growing Kittens:</strong> 2.0-2.5x RER (up to 1 year)</li>
      <li><strong>🤰 Pregnant Females:</strong> 1.5-2.0x RER (increasing throughout pregnancy)</li>
      <li><strong>🍼 Lactating Females:</strong> 2.0-4.0x RER (depending on litter size)</li>

      <li><strong>🏥 Medical Conditions:</strong> Varies based on specific condition</li>

    <ul>

      <li><strong>🩺 Veterinary Supervision:</strong> Always consult vet for significant weight changes</li>

faq:
  - question: How accurate are pet calorie calculators?
    answer: "Pet calorie calculators provide good starting estimates using veterinary-approved formulas. However, individual pets may need 10-20% more or fewer calories than calculated. Monitor your pet's weight and body condition, and adjust portions accordingly."
  - question: How often should I recalculate my pet's calorie needs?
    answer: "Recalculate whenever your pet's weight changes by more than 5%, their activity level changes significantly, or they transition life stages (puppy to adult, adult to senior). Generally, reassess every 3-6 months."
  - question: What if my pet is overweight or underweight?
    answer: "For overweight pets, reduce calories by 20-30% and increase exercise. For underweight pets, increase calories by 20-30%. Always work with your veterinarian for safe and effective weight management plans."
  - question: Are treats included in the daily calorie count?
    answer: "Yes! Treats should count toward your pet's daily calorie allowance and shouldn't exceed 10% of total daily calories. If you give lots of treats, reduce meal portions accordingly to prevent weight gain."
  - question: Do indoor and outdoor pets have different calorie needs?
    answer: "Yes. Outdoor pets typically need 10-25% more calories due to increased activity and temperature regulation. Indoor pets are often more sedentary and may need fewer calories to maintain healthy weight."
  - question: How do spaying/neutering affect calorie needs?
    answer: "Spayed and neutered pets often need 10-20% fewer calories due to reduced metabolism. Monitor weight closely after surgery and adjust food portions to prevent weight gain, which is common post-surgery."
  - question: What about senior pets with reduced activity?
    answer: "Senior pets often need 10-20% fewer calories due to reduced metabolism and activity. However, some seniors need more calories if they're losing muscle mass. Focus on high-quality protein and consult your vet."
  - question: How do I measure food portions accurately?
    answer: "Use a kitchen scale for dry food (most accurate) or a proper measuring cup. Avoid using random cups or bowls. For wet food, check the can/pouch for feeding guidelines and adjust based on your pet's calorie needs."
