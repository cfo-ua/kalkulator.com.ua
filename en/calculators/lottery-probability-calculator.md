  question: Do my odds improve if I buy multiple tickets?
- answer: No difference at all. Every combination has exactly the same probability.
    Quick pick is random, and so is any combination you choose. 1-2-3-4-5-6 has the
    same odds as any other combination.
  question: Are quick pick numbers better than choosing my own?
- answer: Smaller local lotteries and scratch-offs typically have better odds than
    multi-state games like Powerball. However, 'better' odds might be 1 in 1 million
    instead of 1 in 300 million - still extremely unlikely.
  question: What's the best lottery game to play for better odds?
- answer: Bonus numbers multiply the total combinations. For Powerball, you multiply
    the main number combinations (292,201,338) by the Powerball choices (26) to get
    the final odds.
  question: How do bonus numbers affect the probability calculation?
- answer: No. Every drawing is independent and random. Past results don't influence
    future draws. The only way to improve odds is to buy more tickets, but the improvement
    is mathematically insignificant.
  question: Can lottery systems or strategies actually improve my odds?
- answer: Invest in index funds (historical 7% return), high-yield savings accounts
    (4-5%), pay off debt, or build an emergency fund. Even small regular investments
    compound significantly over time.
  question: What should I do with money instead of buying lottery tickets?
- answer: Scratch-offs typically have better odds of winning something, but lower
    maximum prizes. Odds range from 1 in 3 to 1 in 5 for any prize, but jackpot odds
    are still usually worse than 1 in a million.
