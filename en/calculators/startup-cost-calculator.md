  - question: "What costs should I include when calculating startup expenses?"
    answer: "Include one-time setup costs (legal, equipment, initial marketing) and ongoing monthly expenses (salaries, software, rent, marketing). Don't forget professional services, insurance, and a contingency buffer of 10-20%."
  - question: "How much should I budget for a tech startup?"
    answer: "Tech startups typically need $50,000-$500,000+ depending on complexity. SaaS businesses often start with $100,000-$250,000, while hardware or AI startups may require $500,000-$2M+."
  - question: "Should I include founder salaries in startup costs?"
    answer: "Yes, include modest founder salaries if you need income to survive. Many early-stage founders take minimal salaries initially, but plan for living expenses during the critical first 12-24 months."
  - question: "How long should my startup funding last?"
    answer: "Aim for 12-18 months of runway minimum. This gives you time to achieve key milestones, prove traction, and raise additional funding before running out of money."
  - question: "What percentage should I allocate to different cost categories?"
    answer: "Typical allocation: Personnel (60-70%), Technology (10-15%), Marketing (10-20%), Operations (5-10%), Legal/Administrative (5-10%). Adjust based on your specific business model."
  - question: "Should I include equipment purchases or leasing costs?"
    answer: "Include both. Equipment purchases are one-time costs, while leasing creates ongoing monthly expenses. Consider leasing for expensive items to preserve cash flow."
  - question: "How do I estimate marketing costs for a new startup?"
    answer: "Start with 10-20% of projected revenue or $5,000-$20,000 monthly for digital marketing. Include website development, content creation, paid advertising, and marketing tools."
  - question: "What contingency percentage should I add to my calculations?"
    answer: "Add 10-20% contingency to your total estimate. Startups typically face unexpected expenses, delayed timelines, and higher-than-expected costs in various categories."
