- answer: Most successful MCAT test-takers study 300-500 hours over 3-6 months. Pre-med
    students with strong science backgrounds may need 300-400 hours, while career
    changers or those with weaker backgrounds may need 400-500+ hours.
  question: How many hours should I study for the MCAT?
- answer: Aim for 2-4 hours on weekdays and 4-8 hours on weekends, depending on your
    timeline. Consistency is more important than total hours - studying 3 hours daily
    for 4 months beats cramming 8 hours daily for 6 weeks.
  question: What's the ideal daily study schedule for professional exams?
- answer: Take a baseline practice test before starting, then one practice test every
    2-3 weeks during preparation, and weekly practice tests in the final month. This
    helps track progress and identify weak areas.
  question: How often should I take practice tests during preparation?
- answer: 'Use the Pomodoro Technique: 25-50 minute focused study sessions with 5-15
    minute breaks. Longer than 2 hours without breaks leads to diminishing returns.
    Your brain needs recovery time to consolidate information.'
  question: Is it better to study for longer periods or take more frequent breaks?
- answer: Spend 60% of time on weak areas and 40% maintaining strong areas. Improving
    from 40th to 70th percentile is easier than 80th to 90th percentile, but don't
    let strong areas decay completely.
  question: Should I focus on my weak areas or strengthen my strong areas?
- answer: You're ready when practice test scores consistently meet your target for
    2-3 consecutive tests, you've completed content review, and you feel confident
    with the test format and timing.
  question: How do I know if I'm ready to take the exam?
- answer: 'Evaluate your study methods - passive reading isn''t effective. Switch
    to active methods: practice questions, flashcards, teaching concepts aloud. Consider
    getting tutoring or changing prep materials.'
  question: What if I'm not improving despite studying many hours?
- answer: With limited time, focus on high-yield topics and practice questions rather
    than comprehensive content review. Study 1-2 hours on weekdays, 4-6 hours on weekends,
    and extend your timeline to 6-8 months.
