    <h2>Pet Medication Dosage Calculator</h2>
    <p>Calculate safe and accurate medication dosages for your pets with our comprehensive <strong>pet medication dosage calculator</strong>. Get proper dosing recommendations based on your pet's weight for common veterinary medications, supplements, and over-the-counter treatments.</p>

    <h3>💊 How Pet Medication Dosing Works</h3>
    <p>Proper medication dosing for pets is critical for safety and effectiveness:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>⚖️ Weight-Based Calculation:</strong> Most pet medications are dosed by body weight (mg/kg or mg/lb)</li>
      <li><strong>🎯 Species Differences:</strong> Dogs and cats metabolize drugs differently</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Age Considerations:</strong> Puppies, kittens, and seniors may need adjusted doses</li>
      <li><strong>🕐 Dosing Frequency:</strong> Once, twice, or three times daily based on medication</li>
      <li><strong>🏥 Medical Conditions:</strong> Kidney, liver, or heart disease affects dosing</li>
      <li><strong>🔄 Drug Interactions:</strong> Multiple medications can interact</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🔬 Common Pet Medications</h3>
    <p>This calculator includes dosing for commonly prescribed and over-the-counter pet medications:</p>
    
    <h4>🩹 Pain & Inflammation:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Carprofen (Rimadyl):</strong> 2-4 mg/kg twice daily</li>
      <li><strong>Meloxicam (Metacam):</strong> 0.1-0.2 mg/kg once daily</li>
      <li><strong>Gabapentin:</strong> 5-10 mg/kg 2-3 times daily</li>
      <li><strong>Tramadol:</strong> 2-5 mg/kg 2-3 times daily</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>🦠 Antibiotics:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Amoxicillin:</strong> 10-20 mg/kg twice daily</li>
      <li><strong>Cephalexin:</strong> 15-25 mg/kg twice daily</li>
      <li><strong>Enrofloxacin:</strong> 5-10 mg/kg once daily</li>
      <li><strong>Doxycycline:</strong> 5-10 mg/kg twice daily</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>🐛 Parasite Prevention:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Heartgard (Ivermectin):</strong> 6 mcg/kg monthly</li>
      <li><strong>Bravecto (Fluralaner):</strong> 25-56 mg/kg every 12 weeks</li>
      <li><strong>Fenbendazole:</strong> 50 mg/kg daily for 3 days</li>
      <li><strong>Metronidazole:</strong> 10-25 mg/kg twice daily</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>💊 Supplements & OTC:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Glucosamine:</strong> 20-30 mg/kg daily</li>
      <li><strong>Fish Oil (Omega-3):</strong> 75-100 mg/kg daily</li>
      <li><strong>Probiotics:</strong> Follow product guidelines</li>
      <li><strong>Benadryl (Diphenhydramine):</strong> 1-2 mg/kg 2-3 times daily</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>⚠️ Critical Safety Guidelines</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🩺 Veterinary Supervision:</strong> Always consult your vet before giving any medication</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Never Give Human Meds:</strong> Many human medications are toxic to pets</li>
      <li><strong>📏 Accurate Weighing:</strong> Use precise weight measurements</li>
      <li><strong>💊 Proper Dosing Tools:</strong> Use syringes or pill cutters for accuracy</li>
      <li><strong>⏰ Consistent Timing:</strong> Give medications at the same times daily</li>
      <li><strong>🔄 Complete Courses:</strong> Finish entire antibiotic courses</li>
      <li><strong>👀 Monitor for Side Effects:</strong> Watch for adverse reactions</li>
      <li><strong>📞 Emergency Contact:</strong> Know your emergency vet's number</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🚨 Toxic Medications for Pets</h3>
    <p><strong>NEVER give these human medications to pets:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🚫 Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin):</strong> Causes kidney failure and stomach ulcers</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Acetaminophen (Tylenol):</strong> Toxic to cats, liver damage in dogs</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Aspirin:</strong> Can cause bleeding and stomach ulcers</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Naproxen (Aleve):</strong> Extremely toxic, causes kidney failure</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Xylitol (artificial sweetener):</strong> Causes life-threatening hypoglycemia</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Chocolate:</strong> Contains theobromine, toxic to dogs and cats</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📊 Dosing Considerations by Pet Type</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🐕 Dogs:</strong> Generally more tolerant of medications than cats</li>
      <li><strong>🐱 Cats:</strong> Cannot metabolize many drugs, need special formulations</li>
      <li><strong>🐰 Rabbits:</strong> Very sensitive to antibiotics, limited safe options</li>
      <li><strong>🐹 Small Animals:</strong> Require tiny doses, difficult to measure accurately</li>
      <li><strong>🦎 Exotic Pets:</strong> Limited research, require specialized veterinary care</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📱 When to Contact Your Vet</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🚨 Overdose Suspected:</strong> Contact immediately, don't wait</li>
      <li><strong>🤢 Side Effects:</strong> Vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy, loss of appetite</li>
      <li><strong>😵 Allergic Reactions:</strong> Swelling, difficulty breathing, hives</li>
      <li><strong>❓ Dosing Questions:</strong> When unsure about any medication</li>
      <li><strong>💊 Multiple Medications:</strong> Before giving multiple drugs together</li>
    </ul>

faq:
  - question: Can I give my dog human pain medication?
    answer: "No! Human pain medications like ibuprofen, acetaminophen, and naproxen are toxic to pets and can cause organ failure or death. Only give medications specifically prescribed by your veterinarian."
  - question: How do I accurately measure liquid medication for my pet?
    answer: "Use a dosing syringe (without needle) for accurate measurement. Never use kitchen spoons as they vary in size. Most veterinary pharmacies provide measuring syringes with liquid medications."
  - question: What should I do if I accidentally give my pet too much medication?
    answer: "Contact your veterinarian or pet poison control hotline immediately. Don't wait for symptoms to appear. Bring the medication bottle and note the time and amount given."
  - question: Can I split pills to get the right dose for my smaller pet?
    answer: "Only split pills if your veterinarian says it's safe. Some medications have special coatings or time-release formulations that shouldn't be broken. Use a proper pill cutter for accuracy."
  - question: How do I know if my pet is having an allergic reaction to medication?
    answer: "Signs include facial swelling, difficulty breathing, hives, vomiting, diarrhea, or collapse. Stop the medication immediately and contact your veterinarian for emergency care."
  - question: Is it safe to give expired pet medications?
    answer: "No, expired medications can be ineffective or potentially harmful. Some antibiotics become toxic when expired. Always check expiration dates and dispose of old medications properly."
  - question: Can cats take the same medications as dogs?
    answer: "Not always. Cats metabolize drugs differently than dogs and are more sensitive to many medications. Never give dog medication to cats without veterinary approval."
  - question: How do I give medication to a difficult pet?
    answer: "Try hiding pills in treats (pill pockets), mixing liquid medication with food, or asking your vet about flavored formulations. Never force medication down your pet's throat as this can cause choking."
