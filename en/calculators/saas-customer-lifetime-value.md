faq:
  - question: "How do you calculate Customer Lifetime Value for SaaS?"
    answer: "CLV = (ARPU × Gross Margin %) ÷ Monthly Churn Rate. For example: ($100 ARPU × 80% margin) ÷ 3% churn = $2,667 CLV. Some models use Customer Lifespan: CLV = ARPU × Gross Margin % × Average Lifespan in months."
  - question: "What's a good LTV:CAC ratio for SaaS businesses?"
    answer: "A ratio of 3:1 is the minimum for viable unit economics, 5:1+ indicates excellent efficiency. Ratios below 3:1 suggest unsustainable customer acquisition costs or low customer value."
  - question: "How is churn rate calculated for subscription businesses?"
    answer: "Monthly Churn Rate = (Customers lost during month ÷ Customers at start of month) × 100. Annual churn can be calculated as: 1 - (1 - monthly churn rate)^12."
  - question: "Should I use gross revenue or net revenue for CLV?"
    answer: "Use net revenue (after deducting direct service costs) for more accurate profitability analysis. Include costs like hosting, support, and third-party fees but exclude sales and marketing costs."
  - question: "How often should I recalculate CLV for my SaaS business?"
    answer: "Recalculate monthly or quarterly as your business evolves. Key triggers include pricing changes, new features, shifts in churn rates, or entering new customer segments."
  - question: "What factors can increase Customer Lifetime Value?"
    answer: "Reduce churn through better onboarding and support, increase ARPU via upselling/cross-selling, implement annual billing discounts, add premium features, and focus on customer success."
  - question: "How does customer acquisition cost affect profitability?"
    answer: "CAC directly impacts payback period and ROI. Lower CAC or higher CLV improves unit economics. Monitor blended CAC across all channels and organic vs. paid acquisition costs."
  - question: "What's the difference between customer lifespan and retention rate?"
    answer: "Customer lifespan is average time before churn (1 ÷ churn rate). Retention rate is percentage staying active each period. Both are inverse relationships  -  higher retention means longer lifespan."
