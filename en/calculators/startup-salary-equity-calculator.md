    answer: "The most common is 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff. This means no equity vests for the first year, then 25% vests, followed by monthly vesting for the remaining 36 months."
  - question: "How do I value equity for compensation purposes?"
    answer: "Use recent valuation (funding round or 409A valuation) as base case. Model conservative (50% discount), moderate (current value), and optimistic (2-5x growth) scenarios for total compensation calculations."
  - question: "Should I offer more equity or higher salary?"
    answer: "Balance depends on company stage, cash position, and employee risk tolerance. Early-stage startups typically offer lower cash and higher equity. Later-stage companies can offer more competitive salaries."
  - question: "How do I calculate the tax implications of equity compensation?"
    answer: "This calculator doesn't include tax advice. Consult a tax professional. Generally, stock options have tax implications at exercise and sale, while restricted stock is taxed at vesting."
  - question: "What happens to equity when employees leave the company?"
    answer: "Unvested equity typically expires. Vested options usually must be exercised within 90 days of departure, though some companies extend this period. Include clear terms in equity agreements."
  - question: "How does dilution affect employee equity value?"
    answer: "New funding rounds dilute ownership percentages but can increase per-share value. A smaller percentage of a more valuable company can be worth more than a larger percentage of a less valuable company."

    answer: "Standard startup benefits: health insurance ($8,000-$15,000/year value), retirement contributions (3-6% of salary), equity, flexible time off, professional development, and workspace/equipment allowances."
