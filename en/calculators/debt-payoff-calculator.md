    <h2>Debt Payoff Calculator - Choose Your Debt Elimination Strategy</h2>
    <p>Calculate the most effective way to <strong>pay off your debt</strong> using either the debt snowball or debt avalanche method. See exactly how long it will take to become debt-free and how much interest you'll save with different strategies.</p>

    <h3>Debt Payoff Methods Explained:</h3>
    
    <p><strong>🌨️ Debt Snowball Method:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Pay minimums on all debts</li>
      <li>Put extra money toward the <strong>smallest balance first</strong></li>
      <li>Once smallest debt is paid off, roll that payment to the next smallest</li>
      <li><strong>Pros:</strong> Quick wins, psychological motivation, builds momentum</li>
      <li><strong>Cons:</strong> May cost more in interest over time</li>
      <li><strong>Best for:</strong> People who need motivation and quick wins</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>⛰️ Debt Avalanche Method:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Pay minimums on all debts</li>
      <li>Put extra money toward the <strong>highest interest rate first</strong></li>
      <li>Once highest rate debt is paid off, move to next highest rate</li>
      <li><strong>Pros:</strong> Saves the most money in interest, mathematically optimal</li>
      <li><strong>Cons:</strong> May take longer to see first payoff, less motivating</li>
      <li><strong>Best for:</strong> People focused on saving money and math-driven approach</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Which Method Should You Choose?</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Choose Snowball if:</strong> You need motivation, have struggled with debt before, or want quick wins</li>
      <li><strong>Choose Avalanche if:</strong> You're disciplined, want to save the most money, and can stay motivated long-term</li>
      <li><strong>Hybrid approach:</strong> Start with snowball for motivation, then switch to avalanche</li>
      <li><strong>Consider your personality:</strong> Success requires consistency regardless of method</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Additional Debt Payoff Strategies:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Debt Consolidation:</strong> Combine multiple debts into one lower-interest loan</li>
      <li><strong>Balance Transfer:</strong> Move credit card debt to 0% APR promotional offers</li>
      <li><strong>Refinancing:</strong> Replace high-interest loans with lower-rate alternatives</li>
      <li><strong>Side Income:</strong> Use extra earnings exclusively for debt payments</li>
      <li><strong>Sell Assets:</strong> Use proceeds from selling items to pay down debt</li>
      <li><strong>Negotiate:</strong> Contact creditors to request lower interest rates</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Types of Debt to Include:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Credit Cards:</strong> Usually highest interest rates (15-25%)</li>
      <li><strong>Personal Loans:</strong> Fixed rates, typically 6-36%</li>
      <li><strong>Auto Loans:</strong> Secured by vehicle, usually 3-12%</li>
      <li><strong>Student Loans:</strong> Federal (3-7%) or private (variable rates)</li>
      <li><strong>Medical Debt:</strong> Often 0% interest but should be prioritized</li>
      <li><strong>Home Equity Lines:</strong> Variable rates, tax-deductible interest</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Debts to Exclude from This Calculator:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Mortgage:</strong> Usually lowest rate, different payoff strategy</li>
      <li><strong>Business Loans:</strong> May have tax implications</li>
      <li><strong>Investment Property Loans:</strong> Different consideration factors</li>
      <li><strong>401k Loans:</strong> Repaid through payroll, different terms</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Tips for Successful Debt Payoff:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Create a budget:</strong> Know exactly where your money goes each month</li>
      <li><strong>Build emergency fund:</strong> Start with $1,000 to avoid new debt</li>
      <li><strong>Stop using credit:</strong> Switch to cash/debit only during payoff</li>
      <li><strong>Increase income:</strong> Take on extra work or sell unused items</li>
      <li><strong>Reduce expenses:</strong> Cut unnecessary spending temporarily</li>
      <li><strong>Automate payments:</strong> Set up automatic payments to stay on track</li>
      <li><strong>Track progress:</strong> Visual progress charts maintain motivation</li>
      <li><strong>Celebrate milestones:</strong> Acknowledge each debt paid off</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Common Debt Payoff Mistakes:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Only paying minimums:</strong> Extends payoff time dramatically</li>
      <li><strong>Not having a plan:</strong> Random payments are less effective</li>
      <li><strong>Using cards while paying off:</strong> Defeats the purpose</li>
      <li><strong>Ignoring interest rates:</strong> Can cost thousands in unnecessary interest</li>
      <li><strong>Not budgeting:</strong> Hard to find extra money without a plan</li>
      <li><strong>Being too aggressive:</strong> Leaving no room for emergencies</li>
      <li><strong>Giving up too early:</strong> Debt payoff requires persistence</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>When to Consider Professional Help:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Debt exceeds 40% of income:</strong> May need debt management plan</li>
      <li><strong>Unable to make minimums:</strong> Credit counseling can help</li>
      <li><strong>Considering bankruptcy:</strong> Consult attorney first</li>
      <li><strong>Overwhelmed by options:</strong> Financial advisor can provide guidance</li>
      <li><strong>Multiple creditor calls:</strong> Debt management company may help</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Life After Debt Freedom:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Build emergency fund:</strong> 3-6 months of expenses</li>
      <li><strong>Increase retirement savings:</strong> Use freed-up debt payments</li>
      <li><strong>Consider investing:</strong> Put money to work for long-term goals</li>
      <li><strong>Maintain good habits:</strong> Continue budgeting and tracking spending</li>
      <li><strong>Use credit responsibly:</strong> Pay off balances in full each month</li>
    </ul>
