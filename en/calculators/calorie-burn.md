    <h2>Calories Burned Calculator — Accurate Exercise & Activity Calorie Counter</h2>
    <p>This comprehensive <strong>calories burned calculator</strong> helps you determine exactly how many calories you burn during various exercises and daily activities. Based on scientifically validated <strong>MET (Metabolic Equivalent)</strong> values, it provides accurate estimates considering your body weight, activity duration, and exercise intensity.</p>

    <h3>How the Calorie Burn Calculator Works</h3>
    <p>The calculator uses the proven formula: <strong>Calories = MET × Weight (kg) × Duration (hours)</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>MET Value:</strong> Metabolic equivalent representing exercise intensity</li>
      <li><strong>Body Weight:</strong> Heavier individuals burn more calories</li>
      <li><strong>Duration:</strong> Longer activities burn more total calories</li>
      <li><strong>Activity Type:</strong> Different exercises have different calorie burn rates</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Popular Activities & Their Calorie Burn</h3>
    
    <h4>Cardio Exercises (High Calorie Burn)</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Running (6 mph):</strong> 600-900 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Swimming (moderate):</strong> 400-700 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Cycling (12-14 mph):</strong> 500-750 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Rowing machine:</strong> 450-650 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Elliptical trainer:</strong> 400-600 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Jump rope:</strong> 600-900 calories/hour</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>Strength Training & Fitness</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Weight lifting (vigorous):</strong> 360-540 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>CrossFit/HIIT:</strong> 500-800 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Circuit training:</strong> 400-600 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Yoga (vinyasa):</strong> 200-400 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Pilates:</strong> 250-375 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Calisthenics:</strong> 300-450 calories/hour</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>Sports & Recreation</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Basketball:</strong> 400-650 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Soccer/Football:</strong> 450-700 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Tennis:</strong> 350-550 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Rock climbing:</strong> 500-750 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Martial arts:</strong> 400-600 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Dancing (vigorous):</strong> 300-500 calories/hour</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>Daily Activities & Walking</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Walking (3.5 mph):</strong> 250-400 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Hiking:</strong> 350-550 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Gardening:</strong> 200-350 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>House cleaning:</strong> 150-300 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Cooking:</strong> 100-200 calories/hour</li>
      <li><strong>Playing with kids:</strong> 200-350 calories/hour</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Factors Affecting Calorie Burn</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Body Weight:</strong> Heavier people burn more calories performing the same activity</li>
      <li><strong>Muscle Mass:</strong> More muscle tissue increases calorie burn</li>
      <li><strong>Exercise Intensity:</strong> Higher intensity = more calories burned</li>
      <li><strong>Fitness Level:</strong> Trained athletes may have more efficient energy use</li>
      <li><strong>Age & Gender:</strong> Men typically burn more calories than women</li>
      <li><strong>Environmental Factors:</strong> Heat, cold, and altitude can affect calorie burn</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Using Calorie Burn for Weight Management</h3>
    
    <h4>For Weight Loss:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Create a calorie deficit by burning 300-500 extra calories daily</li>
      <li>Combine cardio (calorie burning) with strength training (muscle preservation)</li>
      <li>Track both calories consumed and calories burned</li>
      <li>Aim for 1-2 pounds of weight loss per week (3,500 calories = 1 pound)</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>For Fitness & Health:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Aim for 150+ minutes of moderate exercise weekly (CDC recommendation)</li>
      <li>Include both aerobic activities and strength training</li>
      <li>Gradually increase activity duration and intensity</li>
      <li>Focus on activities you enjoy for long-term adherence</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Exercise Recommendations by Goal</h3>
    
    <h4>Maximum Calorie Burn (Weight Loss Focus):</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>HIIT workouts (20-30 minutes)</li>
      <li>Running or jogging (30-60 minutes)</li>
      <li>Swimming laps (30-45 minutes)</li>
      <li>Cycling intervals (30-60 minutes)</li>
      <li>Circuit training (30-45 minutes)</li>
    </ul>

    <h4>Sustainable Daily Activities:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Brisk walking (45-60 minutes)</li>
      <li>Household chores (60-90 minutes)</li>
      <li>Recreational sports (60 minutes)</li>
      <li>Active hobbies like gardening (60-120 minutes)</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Tips for Accurate Calorie Tracking</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Use a heart rate monitor for more precise measurements</li>
      <li>Track exercise duration accurately (use a timer or fitness app)</li>
      <li>Consider using fitness trackers for all-day activity monitoring</li>
      <li>Log different activities separately for better accuracy</li>
      <li>Account for rest periods during workouts</li>
      <li>Remember that calorie burn continues after intense exercise (EPOC effect)</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Safety & Exercise Guidelines</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Start slowly if you're new to exercise</li>
      <li>Warm up before and cool down after workouts</li>
      <li>Stay hydrated, especially during intense activities</li>
      <li>Listen to your body and rest when needed</li>
      <li>Consult a doctor before starting intense exercise programs</li>
      <li>Focus on proper form to prevent injuries</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Note:</strong> Calorie burn estimates are based on average MET values and may vary between individuals. Factors like fitness level, body composition, and exercise efficiency can affect actual calorie expenditure. Use these estimates as a general guide and adjust based on your personal results and goals.</p>
