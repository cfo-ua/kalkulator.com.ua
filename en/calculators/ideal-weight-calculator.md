    <h2>⚖️ Ideal Weight Calculator - Find Your Perfect Body Weight</h2>
    <p>This <strong>ideal weight calculator</strong> uses multiple proven medical formulas to determine your optimal body weight range. Calculate your target weight using <strong>BMI, Devine, Hamwi, Robinson, and Miller formulas</strong> while considering your height, age, gender, and body frame.</p>

    <h3>📊 Medical Formulas Used</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🏥 BMI Method:</strong> World Health Organization healthy weight range (BMI 18.5-24.9)</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Devine Formula:</strong> Developed for drug dosing, widely used in medical settings</li>
      <li><strong>🔬 Hamwi Formula:</strong> Created for diabetic patients, considers frame size</li>
      <li><strong>📈 Robinson Formula:</strong> Updated version of Devine formula</li>
      <li><strong>🧮 Miller Formula:</strong> Modern adaptation for current populations</li>
      <li><strong>🎯 Body Frame Adjustment:</strong> Small, medium, large frame considerations</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎯 Understanding Ideal Weight Ranges</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>📏 Height-Based:</strong> Formulas calculate weight based on height</li>
      <li><strong>⚖️ Gender-Specific:</strong> Different calculations for men and women</li>
      <li><strong>🦴 Frame Size:</strong> Small/medium/large body frames affect ideal weight</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Age Considerations:</strong> Slight weight increases acceptable with age</li>
      <li><strong>🏋️ Muscle Mass:</strong> Athletes may weigh more due to muscle density</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🔍 Body Frame Size Guide</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>👐 Wrist Measurement Method:</strong> Measure around smallest part of wrist</li>
      <li><strong>Small Frame Men:</strong> Wrist &lt; 6.5 inches (16.5 cm)</li>
      <li><strong>Medium Frame Men:</strong> Wrist 6.5-7.5 inches (16.5-19 cm)</li>
      <li><strong>Large Frame Men:</strong> Wrist &gt; 7.5 inches (19 cm)</li>
      <li><strong>Small Frame Women:</strong> Wrist &lt; 6 inches (15.2 cm)</li>
      <li><strong>Medium Frame Women:</strong> Wrist 6-6.25 inches (15.2-15.9 cm)</li>
      <li><strong>Large Frame Women:</strong> Wrist &gt; 6.25 inches (15.9 cm)</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📈 BMI Categories & Health Implications</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🔵 Underweight:</strong> BMI &lt; 18.5 - May indicate malnutrition</li>
      <li><strong>🟢 Normal Weight:</strong> BMI 18.5-24.9 - Optimal health range</li>
      <li><strong>🟡 Overweight:</strong> BMI 25-29.9 - Increased health risks</li>
      <li><strong>🔴 Obese Class I:</strong> BMI 30-34.9 - Moderate risk</li>
      <li><strong>🟣 Obese Class II:</strong> BMI 35-39.9 - High risk</li>
      <li><strong>⚫ Obese Class III:</strong> BMI ≥ 40 - Very high risk</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎯 Factors Affecting Ideal Weight</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🧬 Genetics:</strong> Family history influences natural weight range</li>
      <li><strong>💪 Muscle Mass:</strong> Athletes and active individuals may weigh more</li>
      <li><strong>🦴 Bone Density:</strong> Varies between individuals and ethnicities</li>
      <li><strong>📅 Age:</strong> Metabolism and muscle mass change over time</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Health Conditions:</strong> Thyroid, hormones affect weight</li>
      <li><strong>💊 Medications:</strong> Some drugs influence weight gain/loss</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🏃‍♀️ Creating a Healthy Weight Plan</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🎯 Realistic Goals:</strong> Aim for 1-2 pounds per week weight loss</li>
      <li><strong>🥗 Balanced Nutrition:</strong> Focus on whole foods, portion control</li>
      <li><strong>🏋️ Regular Exercise:</strong> Combine cardio and strength training</li>
      <li><strong>💧 Hydration:</strong> Drink adequate water daily</li>
      <li><strong>😴 Quality Sleep:</strong> 7-9 hours affects weight regulation</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Progress Tracking:</strong> Monitor weight, measurements, body fat</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>⚠️ When Ideal Weight Calculations May Not Apply</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🏋️‍♂️ Bodybuilders/Athletes:</strong> High muscle mass increases weight</li>
      <li><strong>👵 Elderly:</strong> Some weight gain normal with aging</li>
      <li><strong>🤰 Pregnancy:</strong> Special weight gain guidelines apply</li>
      <li><strong>🦴 Osteoporosis:</strong> Bone density affects calculations</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Medical Conditions:</strong> Consult healthcare providers</li>
      <li><strong>👶 Children/Teens:</strong> Use age-specific growth charts</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🍎 Nutrition Guidelines for Ideal Weight</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🥬 Vegetables:</strong> Fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables</li>
      <li><strong>🍗 Lean Protein:</strong> 0.8-1g per kg body weight daily</li>
      <li><strong>🌾 Whole Grains:</strong> Choose complex carbohydrates</li>
      <li><strong>🥑 Healthy Fats:</strong> Include nuts, avocado, olive oil</li>
      <li><strong>🥛 Calcium Sources:</strong> Dairy or alternatives for bone health</li>
      <li><strong>🚫 Limit Processed Foods:</strong> Reduce sugar, sodium, trans fats</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🏃 Exercise Recommendations</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>💓 Cardio:</strong> 150 minutes moderate intensity per week</li>
      <li><strong>🏋️ Strength Training:</strong> 2-3 times per week, all muscle groups</li>
      <li><strong>🤸 Flexibility:</strong> Daily stretching or yoga</li>
      <li><strong>🚶 Daily Activity:</strong> 8,000-10,000 steps per day</li>
      <li><strong>⏰ Progressive Increase:</strong> Gradually increase intensity/duration</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📊 Monitoring Your Progress</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>⚖️ Weekly Weigh-ins:</strong> Same time, same conditions</li>
      <li><strong>📏 Body Measurements:</strong> Waist, hips, arms, thighs</li>
      <li><strong>📸 Progress Photos:</strong> Visual tracking of changes</li>
      <li><strong>🩺 Body Fat Percentage:</strong> More accurate than weight alone</li>
      <li><strong>💪 Fitness Improvements:</strong> Strength, endurance gains</li>
      <li><strong>😊 Energy Levels:</strong> Overall well-being indicators</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🏥 When to Consult Healthcare Professionals</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Before Major Changes:</strong> Starting new diet/exercise program</li>
      <li><strong>💊 Medical Conditions:</strong> Diabetes, heart disease, thyroid issues</li>
      <li><strong>🤰 Pregnancy Planning:</strong> Achieve healthy weight before conception</li>
      <li><strong>📉 Rapid Weight Changes:</strong> Unexplained weight loss/gain</li>
      <li><strong>🧠 Eating Disorders:</strong> Unhealthy relationship with food/weight</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>💡 Sustainable Weight Management Tips</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🎯 Lifestyle Changes:</strong> Focus on habits, not quick fixes</li>
      <li><strong>🍽️ Mindful Eating:</strong> Pay attention to hunger and fullness cues</li>
      <li><strong>👥 Support System:</strong> Family, friends, or weight loss groups</li>
      <li><strong>📚 Education:</strong> Learn about nutrition and exercise science</li>
      <li><strong>🎉 Celebrate Success:</strong> Acknowledge achievements along the way</li>
      <li><strong>🔄 Long-term Perspective:</strong> Maintenance is as important as weight loss</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Remember:</strong> Ideal weight is a range, not a single number. These calculations provide general guidelines. Your optimal weight may vary based on individual factors. Always consult healthcare professionals for personalized advice.</p>

faq:
  - question: How accurate are ideal weight calculators?
    answer: "Ideal weight calculators provide general guidelines based on medical formulas, but they don't account for individual factors like muscle mass, bone density, or body composition. They're most accurate for average body types and should be used as starting points for health discussions."
  - question: Which ideal weight formula is most accurate?
    answer: "Different formulas work better for different people. BMI method provides ranges, while Devine, Hamwi, Robinson, and Miller formulas give specific targets. Most doctors use multiple methods and consider individual factors rather than relying on one formula."
  - question: Should athletes and muscular people use these calculators?
    answer: "These calculators may not be accurate for athletes or very muscular individuals, as muscle weighs more than fat. Bodybuilders and serious athletes should focus on body fat percentage and performance metrics rather than scale weight."
  - question: How does body frame size affect ideal weight?
    answer: "Body frame size significantly affects ideal weight. People with larger frames naturally carry more weight in bone and muscle mass. Measure your wrist circumference to determine if you have a small, medium, or large frame."
  - question: What's the difference between BMI and other ideal weight formulas?
    answer: "BMI provides a weight range based on height, while formulas like Devine and Hamwi calculate specific target weights. BMI is more forgiving and accounts for natural variation, while other formulas are more precise but potentially restrictive."
  - question: Can ideal weight change with age?
    answer: "Yes, some gradual weight increase with age is normal and healthy. Muscle mass naturally decreases and metabolism slows. Many health experts accept 5-10 pounds of weight gain per decade after age 30 as normal."
  - question: What if my current weight is very different from my ideal weight?
    answer: "Focus on gradual, sustainable changes rather than dramatic weight loss. Aim for 1-2 pounds per week maximum. Large weight differences should be addressed with healthcare provider guidance and realistic timeline expectations."
  - question: Should I aim for the lowest number in my ideal weight range?
    answer: "Not necessarily. The middle to upper range of your ideal weight may be more sustainable and healthy. Very low weights can be difficult to maintain and may not account for muscle mass or natural body variation."
  - question: How important is ideal weight for overall health?
    answer: "While important, ideal weight is just one health indicator. Body fat percentage, fitness level, blood pressure, cholesterol, and overall wellness are equally important. Don't obsess over a specific number on the scale."
  - question: What if I can't reach my calculated ideal weight?
    answer: "Some people may not reach their calculated ideal weight due to genetics, medical conditions, or body composition. Focus on being in a healthy range, maintaining good habits, and feeling your best rather than achieving a specific number."
