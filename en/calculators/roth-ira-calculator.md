    <h2>Roth IRA Calculator - Plan Your Tax-Free Retirement</h2>
    <p>Calculate your <strong>Roth IRA growth</strong> and plan tax-free retirement income. Compare Roth vs Traditional IRA strategies, understand contribution limits, and optimize your retirement savings.</p>

    <div style="background: #d4edda; border: 1px solid #c3e6cb; border-radius: 0.5rem; padding: 1rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-weight: 500; color: #155724;"><strong>💰 Tax-Free Growth:</strong> Roth IRA contributions are made with after-tax dollars, but all growth and qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free!</p>
    </div>

    <h3>Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA:</h3>
    <p><strong>Roth IRA Benefits:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Tax-free growth and qualified withdrawals</li>
      <li>No required minimum distributions (RMDs)</li>
      <li>Contributions can be withdrawn penalty-free anytime</li>
      <li>Estate planning advantages</li>
      <li>Tax diversification in retirement</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Traditional IRA Benefits:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Tax deduction on contributions (if eligible)</li>
      <li>Lower current tax bill</li>
      <li>Good if you expect lower tax bracket in retirement</li>
      <li>Higher contribution limits for catch-up contributions</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Current Roth IRA Contribution Limits:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Under 50:</strong> $7,000 annually (default current limit)</li>
      <li><strong>50 and older:</strong> $8,000 annually (includes $1,000 catch-up)</li>
      <li><strong>Income limits for full contribution:</strong></li>
      <ul>
        <li>Single: $138,000 - $153,000 (current phaseout range)</li>
        <li>Married filing jointly: $218,000 - $228,000 (current phaseout range)</li>
      </ul>
      <li><em>Note: These limits are updated annually by the IRS. Use the calculator's advanced settings to input current year limits.</em></li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Roth IRA Withdrawal Rules:</h3>
    <p><strong>Contributions:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Can be withdrawn tax and penalty-free anytime</li>
      <li>No age restrictions</li>
      <li>Contributions come out first (ordering rules)</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Earnings:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Must be in account for 5 years</li>
      <li>Must be age 59½ for penalty-free withdrawal</li>
      <li>Exceptions: First home ($10,000), education, medical expenses</li>
      <li>10% penalty if withdrawn early without exception</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Roth IRA Strategies:</h3>
    <p><strong>Young Professionals:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Start early to maximize compound growth</li>
      <li>Currently in lower tax bracket</li>
      <li>Decades for tax-free growth</li>
      <li>Consider Roth over Traditional</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>High Earners:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Backdoor Roth IRA conversion</li>
      <li>Mega backdoor Roth (if employer plan allows)</li>
      <li>Tax diversification strategy</li>
      <li>Estate planning benefits</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Pre-Retirees:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Roth conversions during low-income years</li>
      <li>Reduce future RMDs from Traditional accounts</li>
      <li>Create tax-free income stream</li>
      <li>Hedge against higher future tax rates</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Backdoor Roth IRA:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>When to use:</strong> Income exceeds Roth IRA limits</li>
      <li><strong>Process:</strong> Contribute to non-deductible Traditional IRA, then convert</li>
      <li><strong>Requirements:</strong> Minimal Traditional IRA balances to avoid pro-rata rule</li>
      <li><strong>Tax implications:</strong> Pay taxes on any earnings during conversion</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs):</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Roth IRA:</strong> No RMDs during owner's lifetime</li>
      <li><strong>Traditional IRA:</strong> RMDs start at age 73</li>
      <li><strong>Inherited Roth:</strong> 10-year distribution rule for most beneficiaries</li>
      <li><strong>Spouse beneficiary:</strong> Can treat as own account</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Investment Options:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Stocks:</strong> Growth potential for long-term accounts</li>
      <li><strong>Index funds:</strong> Low-cost diversification</li>
      <li><strong>Target-date funds:</strong> Automatic rebalancing</li>
      <li><strong>International exposure:</strong> Global diversification</li>
      <li><strong>Real Estate (REITs):</strong> Alternative asset class</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Common Mistakes to Avoid:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Not starting early:</strong> Missing years of compound growth</li>
      <li><strong>Contribution timing:</strong> Contributing in January vs December</li>
      <li><strong>Income limits:</strong> Exceeding eligibility thresholds</li>
      <li><strong>Early withdrawals:</strong> Taking earnings before 59½</li>
      <li><strong>Not maximizing:</strong> Contributing less than the annual limit</li>
      <li><strong>Poor investments:</strong> Too conservative or too risky for age</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Tax Planning Considerations:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Current vs future tax rates:</strong> Roth better if rates increase</li>
      <li><strong>Tax diversification:</strong> Mix of Roth and Traditional accounts</li>
      <li><strong>State taxes:</strong> Consider state income tax implications</li>
      <li><strong>Conversion opportunities:</strong> Low-income years for conversions</li>
      <li><strong>Estate taxes:</strong> Roth accounts excluded from taxable estate</li>
    </ul>

faq:
  - question: "Should I choose Roth or Traditional IRA?"
    answer: "Choose Roth if you're in a lower tax bracket now than you expect in retirement, or if you value tax-free withdrawals and no RMDs. Choose Traditional if you need the current tax deduction and expect to be in a lower bracket in retirement."
  - question: "Can I contribute to both Roth and Traditional IRA?"
    answer: "Yes, but your total contributions to all IRAs cannot exceed the annual limit ($7,000 current standard limit, $8,000 if 50+). You can split contributions between account types."
  - question: "What is a backdoor Roth IRA?"
    answer: "A strategy for high earners to contribute to a Roth IRA by making a non-deductible Traditional IRA contribution and immediately converting it to Roth. This bypasses the income limits for direct Roth contributions."
  - question: "When can I withdraw money from my Roth IRA?"
    answer: "Contributions can be withdrawn anytime tax and penalty-free. Earnings can be withdrawn tax and penalty-free after age 59½ and the account has been open for 5 years. Some exceptions allow early withdrawal of earnings."
  - question: "How much should I contribute to my Roth IRA?"
    answer: "Ideally, contribute the maximum allowed (currently $7,000 standard limit, $8,000 if 50+). If you can't max out, contribute what you can afford consistently. Even small regular contributions can grow significantly over time."
  - question: "Can I convert my Traditional IRA to Roth?"
    answer: "Yes, you can convert any amount from Traditional to Roth IRA, but you'll owe income tax on the converted amount. This can be beneficial during low-income years or to reduce future RMDs."
  - question: "What happens to my Roth IRA when I die?"
    answer: "Spouses can treat inherited Roth IRAs as their own. Other beneficiaries must generally withdraw the account within 10 years, but withdrawals remain tax-free for qualified distributions."
  - question: "Should I prioritize Roth IRA or 401(k)?"
    answer: "First, contribute enough to your 401(k) to get any employer match. Then consider maxing out your Roth IRA for more investment options and flexibility. Finally, return to your 401(k) to maximize tax-advantaged savings."
