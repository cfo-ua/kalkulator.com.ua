faq:
  - question: How do I know if a name is right for my pet?
    answer: "A good pet name should be easy to pronounce, reflect your pet's personality, and feel natural when you call it. Try saying the name several times and see if it feels right. Your pet should also respond positively to the sound."
  - question: Should I consider my pet's breed when choosing a name?
    answer: "Breed can influence naming - for example, German Shepherd owners might prefer strong names like Rex or Commander, while Poodle owners might choose elegant names like Duchess or Pierre. However, individual personality is more important than breed stereotypes."
  - question: Can I change my pet's name if they're already used to another one?
    answer: "Yes! Pets can learn new names, especially if you're consistent and use positive reinforcement. It typically takes 2-4 weeks for a pet to fully adjust to a new name. Use treats and praise when they respond to their new name."
  - question: Are human names okay for pets?
    answer: "Absolutely! Many pets have human names like Max, Bella, Charlie, or Lucy. Choose whatever feels right for your family. Some people prefer distinctly pet names, while others love giving their pets human names."
  - question: How important is name length for training?
    answer: "Shorter names (1-2 syllables) are generally better for training because they're easier for pets to recognize and for owners to call quickly. However, longer names can work if you use a short nickname for commands."
  - question: Should I avoid names that sound like commands?
    answer: "Yes, avoid names that sound like common commands. For example, 'Ray' sounds like 'stay,' 'Kit' sounds like 'sit,' and 'Noah' sounds like 'no.' This can confuse your pet during training."
  - question: What if my family can't agree on a name?
    answer: "Try creating a shortlist of top names and living with each for a day or two. Often, one name will feel more natural over time. You can also combine names or use voting to reach consensus."
  - question: Are there any names I should definitely avoid?
    answer: "Avoid names that are embarrassing to call in public, sound like commands, are hard to pronounce, or have negative connotations. Also consider how the name will sound when your pet is older - cute puppy names might not suit a senior dog."
