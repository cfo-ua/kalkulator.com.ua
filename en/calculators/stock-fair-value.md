    <h2>Stock Fair Value Calculator - Benjamin Graham's Intelligent Investor Method</h2>
    <p>Calculate stock <strong>intrinsic value</strong> using Benjamin Graham's time-tested methodology from "The Intelligent Investor." This tool applies Graham's conservative approach to determine whether a stock is undervalued, fairly valued, or overvalued.</p>

    <h3>Benjamin Graham's Valuation Methods:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Graham Number</strong> - Conservative fair value based on earnings and book value</li>
      <li><strong>Earnings-Based Valuation</strong> - P/E ratio analysis with growth considerations</li>
      <li><strong>Dividend Discount Model</strong> - Valuation based on dividend yield and growth</li>
      <li><strong>Net-Net Working Capital</strong> - Deep value approach for distressed situations</li>
      <li><strong>Asset-Based Valuation</strong> - Book value and tangible asset analysis</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Graham's Investment Criteria:</h3>
    <p><strong>Financial Strength Requirements:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Earnings stability over the past 10 years</li>
      <li>Dividend payments for at least 20 years (for dividend stocks)</li>
      <li>Earnings growth of at least 1/3 over 10 years</li>
      <li>P/E ratio not exceeding 15x current earnings</li>
      <li>Price-to-book ratio not exceeding 1.5x book value</li>
      <li>Debt-to-equity ratio below 50%</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Quality Indicators:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Consistent profitability and positive cash flow</li>
      <li>Strong balance sheet with adequate working capital</li>
      <li>Reasonable debt levels and interest coverage</li>
      <li>Competitive market position and business moats</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Perfect for Value Investors:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Long-term investors</strong> seeking undervalued opportunities</li>
      <li><strong>Conservative investors</strong> prioritizing capital preservation</li>
      <li><strong>Fundamental analysts</strong> using systematic valuation approaches</li>
      <li><strong>Portfolio managers</strong> screening for value investments</li>
      <li><strong>Individual investors</strong> learning disciplined investment analysis</li>
      <li><strong>Financial advisors</strong> providing objective stock evaluations</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Key Valuation Insights:</h3>
    <p><strong>Graham Number Formula:</strong> √(22.5 × EPS × Book Value per Share). This provides a conservative estimate of fair value based on both earnings power and asset backing.</p>
    
    <p><strong>P/E Ratio Analysis:</strong> Graham preferred P/E ratios below 15x for established companies, with adjustments for growth rates and industry characteristics.</p>
    
    <p><strong>Margin of Safety:</strong> Central to Graham's philosophy - buy only when market price is significantly below calculated intrinsic value (typically 25-50% discount).</p>

    <h3>Investment Decision Framework:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Strong Buy:</strong> Market price 40%+ below calculated fair value</li>
      <li><strong>Buy:</strong> Market price 20-40% below fair value</li>
      <li><strong>Hold:</strong> Market price within ±20% of fair value</li>
      <li><strong>Sell:</strong> Market price 20%+ above fair value</li>
      <li><strong>Avoid:</strong> Fails multiple Graham criteria or financial strength tests</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Understanding Graham's Philosophy:</h3>
    <p><strong>Mr. Market Concept:</strong> Stock prices fluctuate based on emotions rather than fundamentals. Successful investing requires taking advantage of market irrationality while focusing on intrinsic business value.</p>
    
    <p><strong>Circle of Competence:</strong> Invest only in businesses you understand thoroughly. Avoid complex financial instruments and trendy sectors outside your expertise.</p>
    
    <p><strong>Defensive vs. Enterprising:</strong> Choose an investment approach matching your knowledge, time commitment, and risk tolerance. Defensive investors should focus on diversified, high-quality stocks.</p>

    <h3>Risk Considerations:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Industry cycles</strong> - Consider sector-specific risks and economic sensitivity</li>
      <li><strong>Management quality</strong> - Evaluate leadership track record and capital allocation</li>
      <li><strong>Competitive position</strong> - Assess sustainable competitive advantages</li>
      <li><strong>Financial flexibility</strong> - Ensure adequate liquidity for economic downturns</li>
      <li><strong>Valuation limitations</strong> - Models provide estimates, not precise predictions</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Using Your Valuation Results:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Portfolio construction</strong> - Combine with diversification principles</li>
      <li><strong>Entry timing</strong> - Use calculated fair value for buy/sell decisions</li>
      <li><strong>Risk management</strong> - Maintain margin of safety in all purchases</li>
      <li><strong>Performance tracking</strong> - Monitor how valuations evolve with business results</li>
      <li><strong>Continuous learning</strong> - Refine analysis skills through experience</li>
    </ul>

Consider your financial situation, risk tolerance, and investment objectives.</p>
