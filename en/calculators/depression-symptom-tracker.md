    <h2>Depression Symptom Tracker  -  Comprehensive Depression Assessment & Mood Monitoring Tool</h2>
    <p>
      This comprehensive <strong>depression symptom tracker</strong> helps you monitor and assess depression symptoms across emotional, physical, cognitive, and behavioral domains. Track mood patterns, symptom severity, and changes over time while receiving personalized recommendations for depression management and professional support guidance.
    </p>
    
    <h3>How to Use This Depression Assessment Tool</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Answer questions about your <strong>mood and emotional state</strong> over recent weeks</li>
      <li>Evaluate <strong>physical symptoms</strong> and energy levels</li>
      <li>Assess <strong>cognitive changes</strong> in thinking and concentration</li>
      <li>Review <strong>behavioral patterns</strong> and social engagement</li>
      <li>Receive a detailed <strong>depression severity score</strong> and analysis</li>
      <li>Get personalized <strong>management strategies</strong> and professional guidance</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Depression Symptoms Evaluated</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Emotional Symptoms:</strong> Persistent sadness, hopelessness, emptiness, guilt</li>
      <li><strong>Physical Symptoms:</strong> Fatigue, sleep changes, appetite changes, aches</li>
      <li><strong>Cognitive Symptoms:</strong> Concentration problems, memory issues, decision-making</li>
      <li><strong>Behavioral Symptoms:</strong> Social withdrawal, activity avoidance, self-care neglect</li>
      <li><strong>Motivational Symptoms:</strong> Loss of interest, reduced pleasure, low energy</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Understanding Depression Severity Levels</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Minimal (0-9):</strong> Few or no depression symptoms present</li>
      <li><strong>Mild (10-16):</strong> Some depression symptoms with minimal impact</li>
      <li><strong>Moderate (17-23):</strong> Notable depression symptoms affecting daily life</li>
      <li><strong>Moderately Severe (24-30):</strong> Significant depression requiring attention</li>
      <li><strong>Severe (31+):</strong> Severe depression requiring immediate professional help</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Scientific Basis of Assessment</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>PHQ-9 Elements:</strong> Patient Health Questionnaire depression screening</li>
      <li><strong>DSM-5 Criteria:</strong> Diagnostic manual symptom criteria for depression</li>
      <li><strong>Beck Depression Inventory:</strong> Validated depression assessment components</li>
      <li><strong>Hamilton Rating Scale:</strong> Clinical depression rating elements</li>
      <li><strong>Evidence-Based:</strong> Scientifically validated depression measures</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Major Depression Warning Signs</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Persistent sad, anxious, or empty mood lasting weeks</li>
      <li>Loss of interest or pleasure in activities once enjoyed</li>
      <li>Significant weight loss or gain, appetite changes</li>
      <li>Sleep disturbances (insomnia or sleeping too much)</li>
      <li>Fatigue or loss of energy nearly every day</li>
      <li>Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt</li>
      <li>Difficulty concentrating or making decisions</li>
      <li>Thoughts of death or suicide</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Types of Depression</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Major Depressive Disorder:</strong> Persistent depressive episodes affecting daily life</li>
      <li><strong>Persistent Depressive Disorder:</strong> Long-term, chronic depression symptoms</li>
      <li><strong>Seasonal Affective Disorder:</strong> Depression related to seasonal changes</li>
      <li><strong>Postpartum Depression:</strong> Depression following childbirth</li>
      <li><strong>Bipolar Depression:</strong> Depressive episodes in bipolar disorder</li>
      <li><strong>Atypical Depression:</strong> Depression with unique symptom patterns</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Depression Treatment Approaches</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Psychotherapy:</strong> Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Interpersonal Therapy</li>
      <li><strong>Medication:</strong> Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, others)</li>
      <li><strong>Lifestyle Changes:</strong> Exercise, nutrition, sleep hygiene, stress management</li>
      <li><strong>Social Support:</strong> Family therapy, support groups, peer connections</li>
      <li><strong>Alternative Therapies:</strong> Mindfulness, meditation, creative therapies</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Self-Care Strategies for Depression</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Daily Structure:</strong> Regular routines, goal setting, activity scheduling</li>
      <li><strong>Physical Activity:</strong> Regular exercise, outdoor time, movement practices</li>
      <li><strong>Social Connection:</strong> Maintaining relationships, seeking support, communication</li>
      <li><strong>Mindfulness:</strong> Present-moment awareness, acceptance, meditation</li>
      <li><strong>Healthy Habits:</strong> Nutrition, sleep, substance avoidance, self-compassion</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Depression and Physical Health</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Immune System:</strong> Depression can weaken immune response</li>
      <li><strong>Cardiovascular:</strong> Increased risk of heart disease and stroke</li>
      <li><strong>Chronic Pain:</strong> Depression and pain often co-occur</li>
      <li><strong>Digestive Issues:</strong> Appetite changes, digestive problems</li>
      <li><strong>Sleep Disorders:</strong> Insomnia, sleep quality, circadian rhythms</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Supporting Someone with Depression</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Listen without judgment:</strong> Provide emotional support and validation</li>
      <li><strong>Encourage treatment:</strong> Help connect with professional resources</li>
      <li><strong>Stay connected:</strong> Regular check-ins, social inclusion</li>
      <li><strong>Learn about depression:</strong> Understand symptoms and treatment</li>
      <li><strong>Take care of yourself:</strong> Caregiver self-care and support</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Depression Myths vs. Facts</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Myth:</strong> Depression is just sadness. <strong>Fact:</strong> Depression is a complex medical condition</li>
      <li><strong>Myth:</strong> You can "snap out of it." <strong>Fact:</strong> Depression requires proper treatment</li>
      <li><strong>Myth:</strong> Medication is the only solution. <strong>Fact:</strong> Multiple effective treatments exist</li>
      <li><strong>Myth:</strong> Depression is a sign of weakness. <strong>Fact:</strong> Depression is a health condition</li>
      <li><strong>Myth:</strong> Therapy doesn't work. <strong>Fact:</strong> Therapy is highly effective for depression</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>When to Seek Emergency Help</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Thoughts of suicide or self-harm</li>
      <li>Plans or means to hurt yourself</li>
      <li>Feeling like you might act on harmful thoughts</li>
      <li>Substance abuse to cope with depression</li>
      <li>Complete inability to function in daily life</li>
      <li>Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations, delusions)</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Crisis Resources:</strong> National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988 | Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 | Emergency Services: 911</p>

    <p><strong>Important Disclaimer:</strong> This depression symptom tracker is for educational and self-monitoring purposes only. It is not a diagnostic tool and cannot replace professional mental health evaluation. If you are experiencing thoughts of self-harm or significant depression symptoms, please seek immediate professional help from a qualified healthcare provider or mental health professional.</p>

faq:
  - question: Is this depression tracker a substitute for professional diagnosis?
    answer: "No, this tracker is for self-monitoring and education only. It cannot diagnose depression or replace professional evaluation. If you're experiencing persistent depression symptoms, thoughts of self-harm, or significant life impairment, please consult a mental health professional for proper assessment and treatment."
  - question: How often should I use this depression symptom tracker?
    answer: "Weekly tracking is often most helpful for monitoring patterns and changes over time. Daily tracking can be useful during treatment or crisis periods, but avoid obsessive monitoring which could increase anxiety. The goal is to gain insight into your mental health patterns, not to become fixated on daily fluctuations."
  - question: What should I do if my depression score is high?
    answer: "High scores suggest you may be experiencing significant depression symptoms. This is a signal to seek professional help from a therapist, counselor, or your primary care physician. Don't wait for symptoms to worsen - early intervention leads to better outcomes."
  - question: Can this tracker help me communicate with my healthcare provider?
    answer: "Yes, tracking your symptoms over time provides valuable information for healthcare providers. Bring your tracking results to appointments to help your doctor or therapist understand your symptom patterns, triggers, and how you're responding to treatment."
  - question: What if I'm having thoughts of self-harm?
    answer: "If you're having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, seek immediate help. Contact the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline (988), Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), or emergency services (911). These feelings are treatable, and help is available 24/7."
  - question: How can I improve my depression symptoms between professional visits?
    answer: "While professional treatment is essential, self-care activities can help: maintain regular sleep schedules, engage in physical activity, practice stress management, stay connected with supportive people, limit alcohol, and engage in meaningful activities. However, don't rely solely on self-help for significant depression."
  - question: Can depression symptoms fluctuate significantly?
    answer: "Yes, depression symptoms often fluctuate daily or weekly. Factors like sleep, stress, hormones, season, and life events can influence symptoms. This is why tracking over time is more meaningful than single assessments. Look for overall patterns rather than day-to-day variations."
  - question: What's the difference between sadness and clinical depression?
    answer: "Sadness is a normal emotion that usually passes within days and doesn't significantly impair functioning. Clinical depression involves persistent symptoms lasting two weeks or more that interfere with daily life, work, relationships, and self-care. Depression affects thinking, energy, appetite, sleep, and overall functioning."
  - question: Should family members or friends use this tracker?
    answer: "If you're concerned about a loved one, this tracker can help you understand depression symptoms and encourage them to seek help. However, don't try to diagnose others. Instead, express care, offer support, help them connect with professional resources, and take any suicide threats seriously."
