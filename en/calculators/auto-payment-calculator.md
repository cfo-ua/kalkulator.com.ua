    <h2>Auto Payment Calculator - Calculate Your Car Loan Payments</h2>
    <p>Calculate your <strong>monthly auto loan payments</strong> and compare financing options. Analyze lease vs buy decisions, different loan terms, and find the most affordable car financing for your budget.</p>

    <div style="background: #e8f4fd; border: 1px solid #bee5eb; border-radius: 0.5rem; padding: 1rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
      <p style="margin: 0; font-weight: 500; color: #0c5460;"><strong>🚗 Smart Car Buying:</strong> Use this calculator to determine what car payment fits your budget before shopping. A good rule of thumb is to keep total transportation costs under 20% of income.</p>

    <h3>Auto Financing Options:</h3>
    <p><strong>Dealership Financing:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Convenient one-stop shopping</li>
      <li>Manufacturer incentives and rebates</li>
      <li>Special promotional rates (0% APR offers)</li>
      <li>May mark up rates for profit</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Bank/Credit Union Loans:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Competitive interest rates</li>
      <li>Pre-approval for negotiating power</li>
      <li>Direct relationship with lender</li>
      <li>Better rates for existing customers</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Online Lenders:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Quick approval process</li>
      <li>Competitive rates</li>
      <li>Direct funding</li>
      <li>Limited local support</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Lease vs Buy Analysis:</h3>
    <p><strong>Leasing Pros:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Lower monthly payments</li>
      <li>Always under warranty</li>
      <li>Latest technology and features</li>
      <li>No depreciation risk</li>
      <li>Lower down payment</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Leasing Cons:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>No ownership/equity building</li>
      <li>Mileage restrictions (10,000-15,000/year)</li>
      <li>Wear and tear charges</li>
      <li>Continuous payments</li>
      <li>Early termination penalties</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Buying Pros:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Build equity and ownership</li>
      <li>No mileage restrictions</li>
      <li>Modify vehicle as desired</li>
      <li>No ongoing payments after loan payoff</li>
      <li>Can sell anytime</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Buying Cons:</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Higher monthly payments</li>
      <li>Depreciation loss</li>
      <li>Maintenance costs after warranty</li>
      <li>Higher down payment typically required</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Typical Auto Loan Rate Ranges:</h3>
    <p><em>Note: Rates change frequently based on Federal Reserve policy and market conditions. Check with lenders for current rates.</em></p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>New cars (excellent credit):</strong> 5.5% - 7.5% (example range)</li>
      <li><strong>New cars (good credit):</strong> 7.5% - 10.5% (example range)</li>
      <li><strong>Used cars (excellent credit):</strong> 6.5% - 9.5% (example range)</li>
      <li><strong>Used cars (good credit):</strong> 9.5% - 15.5% (example range)</li>
      <li><strong>Credit union rates:</strong> Often 1-2% lower than banks</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Factors Affecting Auto Loan Rates:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Credit Score:</strong> 750+ gets best rates, 600- may need subprime</li>
      <li><strong>Vehicle Age:</strong> Newer cars get better rates</li>
      <li><strong>Loan Term:</strong> Shorter terms typically have lower rates</li>
      <li><strong>Down Payment:</strong> Larger down payment can improve rates</li>
      <li><strong>Income/DTI:</strong> Debt-to-income ratio affects approval</li>
      <li><strong>Vehicle Type:</strong> Luxury cars may have higher rates</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Auto Loan Terms:</h3>
    <p><strong>36 months (3 years):</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Lowest interest rates</li>
      <li>Highest monthly payments</li>
      <li>Least total interest paid</li>
      <li>Good for reliable used cars</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>48-60 months (4-5 years):</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Balanced payment and interest</li>
      <li>Most popular term length</li>
      <li>Good for new and late-model used cars</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>72-84 months (6-7 years):</strong></p>
    <ul>
      <li>Lowest monthly payments</li>
      <li>Higher interest rates</li>
      <li>Risk of being "upside down" on loan</li>
      <li>Vehicle may need major repairs before payoff</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Total Cost of Ownership:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Purchase price/payments</strong></li>
      <li><strong>Insurance:</strong> Varies by vehicle value and type</li>
      <li><strong>Fuel costs:</strong> Consider MPG and driving habits</li>
      <li><strong>Maintenance:</strong> Scheduled services and repairs</li>
      <li><strong>Registration/taxes:</strong> Annual state fees</li>
      <li><strong>Depreciation:</strong> Vehicle value loss over time</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Negotiating Tips:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Get pre-approved:</strong> Know your rate before shopping</li>
      <li><strong>Negotiate separately:</strong> Price, trade-in, financing separately</li>
      <li><strong>Shop around:</strong> Compare dealership vs bank financing</li>
      <li><strong>Read carefully:</strong> Check for added products and fees</li>
      <li><strong>Consider timing:</strong> End of month/year for better deals</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>When to Refinance:</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Credit improved:</strong> Qualified for better rates</li>
      <li><strong>Market rates dropped:</strong> 1%+ improvement available</li>
      <li><strong>Income changed:</strong> Need lower payments</li>
      <li><strong>Vehicle value:</strong> Not upside down on current loan</li>
    </ul>

faq:
  - question: "What's a good car payment amount?"
    answer: "Financial experts recommend keeping total transportation costs (payment, insurance, gas, maintenance) under 15-20% of your gross monthly income. For payments alone, aim for 10-15% of monthly income."
  - question: "Should I lease or buy a car?"
    answer: "Buy if you drive more than 15,000 miles/year, want to build equity, or plan to keep the car long-term. Lease if you want lower payments, always want a newer car, and stay within mileage limits."
  - question: "What loan term should I choose?"
    answer: "Choose the shortest term you can afford. While longer terms lower monthly payments, they cost more in total interest and risk leaving you upside down on the loan."
  - question: "How much should I put down on a car?"
    answer: "For new cars, put down at least 20%. For used cars, 10% minimum. A larger down payment reduces monthly payments and helps avoid being upside down immediately."
  - question: "Can I get an auto loan with bad credit?"
    answer: "Yes, but expect higher interest rates (15-25%+) and possibly required down payments. Consider credit unions, which often have more flexible lending criteria."
  - question: "What's the difference between APR and interest rate?"
    answer: "Interest rate is the cost of borrowing. APR includes interest plus fees, giving you a more complete picture of the loan's total cost. Always compare APRs when shopping."
  - question: "Should I refinance my auto loan?"
    answer: "Consider refinancing if you can get a rate 1%+ lower than your current rate, your credit has improved, or you need to lower monthly payments. Avoid extending the term significantly."
  - question: "What happens if I'm upside down on my car loan?"
    answer: "Being upside down means you owe more than the car's worth. Options include making extra payments toward principal, waiting for the balance to match value, or gap insurance to cover the difference."
