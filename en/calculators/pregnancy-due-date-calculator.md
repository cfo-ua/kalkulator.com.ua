    <h2>👶 Pregnancy Due Date Calculator - Plan Your Baby's Arrival</h2>
    <p>This <strong>pregnancy due date calculator</strong> helps expectant parents calculate their baby's estimated due date using either the <strong>last menstrual period (LMP) or conception date</strong>. Get detailed pregnancy timeline, current week information, and important milestone dates.</p>

    <h3>📅 How Due Date Calculation Works</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🗓️ Naegele's Rule:</strong> Standard method adding 280 days to LMP</li>
      <li><strong>📊 280-Day Gestation:</strong> Average pregnancy length is 40 weeks</li>
      <li><strong>🎯 LMP Method:</strong> Most common calculation method used by doctors</li>
      <li><strong>🧬 Conception Method:</strong> Adds 266 days from fertilization date</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Medical Accuracy:</strong> Used by healthcare professionals worldwide</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🕐 Pregnancy Timeline & Trimesters</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🌱 First Trimester:</strong> Weeks 1-12 (0-12 weeks)</li>
      <li><strong>🌿 Second Trimester:</strong> Weeks 13-27 (13-27 weeks)</li>
      <li><strong>🌳 Third Trimester:</strong> Weeks 28-40+ (28+ weeks)</li>
      <li><strong>📏 Full Term:</strong> 37-42 weeks considered normal delivery range</li>
      <li><strong>⏰ Early Term:</strong> 37-38 weeks</li>
      <li><strong>🎯 Term:</strong> 39-40 weeks (optimal delivery window)</li>
      <li><strong>⏱️ Late Term:</strong> 41-42 weeks</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎯 Important Pregnancy Milestones</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Week 4:</strong> Missed period, pregnancy test positive</li>
      <li><strong>Week 6-8:</strong> First prenatal appointment, heartbeat detection</li>
      <li><strong>Week 10-13:</strong> NT scan, early genetic screening</li>
      <li><strong>Week 16-20:</strong> Anatomy scan, gender reveal possible</li>
      <li><strong>Week 20:</strong> Halfway point, detailed ultrasound</li>
      <li><strong>Week 24:</strong> Viability milestone (survival outside womb)</li>
      <li><strong>Week 28:</strong> Third trimester begins, glucose test</li>
      <li><strong>Week 32:</strong> Baby's lungs developing rapidly</li>
      <li><strong>Week 36:</strong> Baby considered late preterm if born</li>
      <li><strong>Week 37:</strong> Full-term pregnancy begins</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🔬 Factors Affecting Due Date Accuracy</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🎯 Accuracy Rate:</strong> Only 5% of babies born on exact due date</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Normal Range:</strong> 37-42 weeks is considered full term</li>
      <li><strong>👩 Individual Variation:</strong> Cycle length affects LMP calculations</li>
      <li><strong>🧬 Genetic Factors:</strong> Family history of pregnancy length</li>
      <li><strong>👶 First vs. Subsequent:</strong> First babies often arrive later</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Medical Factors:</strong> Conditions can affect delivery timing</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📋 Prenatal Appointment Schedule</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Weeks 4-28:</strong> Monthly appointments</li>
      <li><strong>Weeks 28-36:</strong> Bi-weekly appointments</li>
      <li><strong>Weeks 36-40:</strong> Weekly appointments</li>
      <li><strong>Post-40 weeks:</strong> Twice weekly monitoring</li>
      <li><strong>Key Tests:</strong> Blood work, ultrasounds, glucose screening</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎯 When to Use Each Calculation Method</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>📅 LMP Method:</strong> Most common, use if you know your last period</li>
      <li><strong>🧬 Conception Method:</strong> Use if you know exact fertilization date</li>
      <li><strong>🔬 IVF Pregnancies:</strong> Use conception date (embryo transfer + 14 days)</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Irregular Cycles:</strong> Ultrasound dating may be more accurate</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Medical Confirmation:</strong> Early ultrasound provides most accurate dating</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>💡 Pregnancy Planning Tips</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>📱 Track Your Cycle:</strong> Know your average cycle length</li>
      <li><strong>🍼 Prenatal Vitamins:</strong> Start before conception if possible</li>
      <li><strong>⚕️ Preconception Health:</strong> Schedule checkup before trying</li>
      <li><strong>🚭 Lifestyle Changes:</strong> Quit smoking, limit alcohol</li>
      <li><strong>📚 Education:</strong> Read about pregnancy and childbirth</li>
      <li><strong>💰 Financial Planning:</strong> Budget for baby expenses</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎉 Baby Development by Trimester</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🌱 First Trimester:</strong> Organ formation, neural tube development</li>
      <li><strong>🌿 Second Trimester:</strong> Gender development, movement begins</li>
      <li><strong>🌳 Third Trimester:</strong> Rapid growth, lung maturation</li>
      <li><strong>🧠 Brain Development:</strong> Continues throughout pregnancy</li>
      <li><strong>👁️ Sensory Development:</strong> Hearing, vision develop progressively</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>⚕️ When to Contact Your Doctor</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🚨 Severe cramping or bleeding</strong></li>
      <li><strong>🤢 Severe nausea preventing eating/drinking</strong></li>
      <li><strong>🔥 Fever over 101°F (38.3°C)</strong></li>
      <li><strong>💧 Signs of preterm labor before 37 weeks</strong></li>
      <li><strong>🩸 Unusual discharge or bleeding</strong></li>
      <li><strong>🤕 Severe headaches or vision changes</strong></li>
    </ul>

    <h3>📊 Due Date Statistics</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>📅 Exact Due Date:</strong> Only 4-5% of babies born on due date</li>
      <li><strong>📈 Within 1 Week:</strong> ~25% born within 1 week of due date</li>
      <li><strong>📊 Within 2 Weeks:</strong> ~70% born within 2 weeks of due date</li>
      <li><strong>👶 First Babies:</strong> Often 1-2 days late on average</li>
      <li><strong>🔄 Subsequent Babies:</strong> Tend to arrive closer to due date</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>🎒 Preparing for Baby's Arrival</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>🏥 Hospital Bag:</strong> Pack around week 36</li>
      <li><strong>🚗 Car Seat:</strong> Install and inspect before delivery</li>
      <li><strong>👶 Nursery Setup:</strong> Complete by week 35</li>
      <li><strong>📋 Birth Plan:</strong> Discuss preferences with care provider</li>
      <li><strong>👥 Support Team:</strong> Arrange help for first weeks home</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Remember:</strong> Due dates are estimates. Every pregnancy is unique, and babies arrive when they're ready. Always consult with your healthcare provider for personalized medical advice.</p>

faq:
  - question: How accurate is the pregnancy due date calculator?
    answer: "Due date calculators are estimates based on average pregnancy length (280 days from LMP). Only 4-5% of babies are born on their exact due date. Most babies (70%) are born within 2 weeks of the estimated due date."
  - question: What's the difference between LMP and conception date methods?
    answer: "LMP method adds 280 days to your last menstrual period - this is the standard medical approach. Conception method adds 266 days to fertilization date. LMP is more commonly used because most people remember their last period better than conception date."
  - question: When should I have my first prenatal appointment?
    answer: "Schedule your first prenatal appointment around 8-10 weeks of pregnancy (6-8 weeks after your missed period). Earlier appointments may be needed if you have medical conditions or pregnancy complications."
  - question: Can my due date change during pregnancy?
    answer: "Yes, early ultrasounds (before 20 weeks) can provide more accurate dating and may adjust your due date. This is especially common if you have irregular cycles or uncertain LMP dates."
  - question: What if I don't remember my last menstrual period?
    answer: "If you don't remember your LMP or have irregular cycles, your doctor will use early ultrasound measurements to estimate your due date. The conception date method can also be used if you know when fertilization occurred."
  - question: Is it normal for babies to be born before or after the due date?
    answer: "Absolutely! Full-term pregnancy is 37-42 weeks. About 80% of babies are born between 38-42 weeks. Early term is 37-38 weeks, and late term is 41-42 weeks - all considered normal."
  - question: How do twins or multiples affect due date calculations?
    answer: "The calculation method is the same, but twins/multiples typically arrive earlier. Average delivery is around 37 weeks for twins and 35 weeks for triplets. Your doctor will monitor you more closely."
  - question: What factors can affect when my baby arrives?
    answer: "Factors include: whether it's your first baby (tend to be late), family history, stress levels, mother's age, medical conditions, baby's position, and natural variation in pregnancy length."
  - question: Should I plan important events around my due date?
    answer: "Plan important events outside the 2-week window before and after your due date. Avoid scheduling non-essential activities from week 38-42, as this is when most babies arrive."
  - question: What if I go past my due date?
    answer: "Going 1-2 weeks past your due date is normal. Your doctor will monitor you more closely after 40 weeks and may discuss induction around 41-42 weeks, depending on your and baby's health."
